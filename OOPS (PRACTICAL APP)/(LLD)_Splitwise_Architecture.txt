💸 LLD Block: The Splitwise Architecture
Designing Splitwise is one of the most common Low-Level Design questions at Amazon and Uber.
The interviewer doesn't care if you know how to build a User class.
They care about the core algorithmic engine: Debt Simplification.


🛑 The Problem
If Alice owes Bob $10, Bob owes Charlie $10, and Charlie owes Alice $10,
a naive system will tell them to make three separate bank transfers.
A Senior Engineer realizes that $10 is moving in a circle. The optimal number of transactions is Zero.


🧱 The Architecture (How to solve it in $O(N \log N)$)

Step 1: The HashMap (Net Balances)
You do not track individual IOU edges between people. 
You reduce everyone to a single number: their Net Balance.
If Alice pays $30 for a meal with Bob and Charlie ($10 each).
Alice's Net Balance: $+20$ (She should receive money)
Bob's Net Balance: $-10$ (He owes money)
Charlie's Net Balance: $-10$ (He owes money)
Sanity Check: The sum of all net balances must always equal $0$.


Step 2: The Two Heaps (Max-Heaps)
You take the HashMap and split the users into two Priority Queues (Max-Heaps):
Creditors (Receivers): Ordered by who is owed the most money.
Debtors (Payers): Ordered by who owes the most money.


Step 3: The Greedy Settlement AlgorithmYou pop the top person off both heaps.
The biggest Debtor pays the biggest Creditor.
You subtract the settled amount from both their balances.
If someone's balance is not yet zero, you push them back into the heap.
You repeat this until both heaps are empty.
